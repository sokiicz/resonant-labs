COVID-19 Timelapse

The whole pandemic as one continuous flight around the Earth, week by week: from the first reported cases in Wuhan to the years when reporting faded.

🗓️ Started Nov 2026 🚧 Not public yet 🤖 Built with Claude Code 🌍 3D globe · Blender · three.js

What is it?

A 3D globe that plays the COVID-19 pandemic from December 2019 onward, as one continuous flight around the Earth. You see where the disease spread and where people died. In the interactive globe you can also switch on how strict the measures were and how vaccination grew. In the end, reporting fades.

COVID-19 didn't disappear. The ending flies over the countries that still reported the most cases in the last weeks of the data.

How to read the globe

  • Cases: countries glow red. The brighter, the more reported cases per million that week.
  • Deaths: white columns. The height follows the reported deaths that week.
  • Measures: borders glow icy blue. The brighter, the stricter. This data ends in 2022 (interactive globe).
  • Vaccination: countries fill teal by the share of people with at least one dose (interactive globe).
  • Fading: a country that stops reporting turns ash grey.

Two forms

A video, and an interactive globe in the browser: fly anywhere while time keeps running, click a country or region for its statistics, and compare a group of countries without the extremes of the rest of the world.

Status

In progress and not public yet. The data, the texts and the country notes are being checked first.

The idea

Not chapters, not a dashboard: one nonstop timelapse of the whole Earth, with the camera following where things were happening, from China to Europe, across the Atlantic and back.

Honest limits

Reported cases depend on how much a country tested. Reported deaths compare better, but they miss many deaths too: WHO estimates 14.9 million excess deaths in 2020 and 2021. From 2023 on, fewer and fewer countries report at all. The globe shows that as greying, not as the end of the disease.

The visuals are meant to be clear and memorable, but they don't bend the numbers: the column height is proportional to the reported deaths.

Behind every column is a person who died; the numbers are reported counts, not the full story of the loss.
